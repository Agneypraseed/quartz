# Personal Curriculum

This page is the personal foundation beneath the academic curriculum. Progress is not only what you learn or build; it is also the quality of the life that supports the work.

Return to the [main curriculum](/curriculum) or the [curated bookmarks](/curriculum/bookmarks).

## The five principles

1. **Meet basic needs.**
2. **Avoid cheap dopamine.**
3. **Leave the past alone.**
4. **Limit desires to those achievable at the edge of your capability.**
5. **Find something beyond yourself** — mission, children, God, community, or service.

---

## 1 — Meet basic needs

Treat the body and immediate environment as infrastructure, not as interruptions to meaningful work.

### Daily baseline

- [ ] Sleep and wake at a sustainable time
- [ ] Eat, hydrate, and take necessary medication or supplements
- [ ] Move, stretch, or spend time outside
- [ ] Maintain hygiene and a usable living space
- [ ] Make space for at least one real human connection
- [ ] Address urgent health, safety, or financial needs before optional optimization

**Question:** What unmet basic need is making everything else harder today?

---

## 2 — Avoid cheap dopamine

Protect attention from rewards that are immediate, effortless, and difficult to stop. Entertainment is not the enemy; unconscious consumption is.

### Attention rules

- [ ] Decide what the current session is for before opening a device
- [ ] Complete one meaningful block of work before algorithmic entertainment
- [ ] Keep notifications and distracting feeds away during focused work
- [ ] Consume deliberately: choose the video, article, game, or conversation in advance
- [ ] Stop when the planned time ends instead of waiting to feel finished

**Question:** Is this activity restoring me, connecting me, or merely helping me avoid discomfort?

---

## 3 — Leave the past alone

The past may contain information, responsibility, and grief, but it cannot become today's workspace forever.

### Processing rule

When something from the past returns, identify which response it requires:

- **Repair:** apologize, repay, correct, or have the necessary conversation
- **Learn:** write the lesson and the behavior that changes next time
- **Grieve:** allow the feeling without turning it into a permanent identity
- **Release:** stop rehearsing what no longer permits useful action

**Question:** Is there a concrete action available now? If not, what would releasing this look like for today?

---

## 4 — Work at the edge of capability

Choose desires that require growth but remain close enough to reality that consistent effort can reach them. Avoid both comfort-only goals and fantasies with no actionable path.

### Goal filter

Before accepting a goal, ask:

- [ ] Can I describe the next physical action?
- [ ] Can I practice the relevant skill every week?
- [ ] Is the difficulty stretching me without depending on constant crisis?
- [ ] Will progress produce evidence within four to six weeks?
- [ ] Am I willing to accept the cost and tradeoffs?

Keep one major stretch goal active at a time. Break it into the smallest project that still tests the capability you want to develop.

**Question:** What is just beyond my present ability but reachable through the next cycle of deliberate practice?

---

## 5 — Find something beyond yourself

Let learning and achievement serve a commitment larger than status or self-image. This may be a mission, family, faith, community, craft, or responsibility to future people.

### Practice

- [ ] Name the people or purpose your work is meant to serve
- [ ] Perform one act of service each week without optimizing it for recognition
- [ ] Keep one relationship that matters protected from productivity pressure
- [ ] Revisit whether your goals still express your values
- [ ] Make room for gratitude, prayer, reflection, or silence

**Question:** Who benefits if I become more capable, and how can they benefit this week rather than someday?

---

## Daily check-in

- **Body:** What do I need physically?
- **Attention:** What deserves my best focus today?
- **Past:** What can I repair, learn from, or release?
- **Edge:** What is today's deliberate stretch?
- **Beyond self:** Who or what does today's work serve?

## Weekly review

- [ ] Which basic need was neglected?
- [ ] Where did distraction become avoidance?
- [ ] What from the past still requires action—and what requires release?
- [ ] Did the week's challenge sit at the edge of my capability?
- [ ] Did my work serve something beyond myself?
- [ ] What is the single adjustment for next week?

> [!note] Standard
> This is a compass, not a scorecard. Use it to notice drift and choose the next honest action, not to manufacture guilt.
